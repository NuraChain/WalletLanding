CertiK counted 52 "wrench attacks" worldwide in the first half of 2026, up from 39 in the same period of 2025 — a rise of 33%, as The Record reported on 24 July. The term covers physical coercion: in CertiK's words, cases "in which adversaries use violence, intimidation or credible threats to compel a victim to transfer digital assets, surrender private keys, unlock a wallet, reveal credentials, or pressure a third party into compliance." They are home invasions and kidnappings and, in rare cases, murder.

Estimates of the money involved vary widely with the method, and CertiK itself cautions that its figure measures scale rather than what criminals actually kept. The count is the firmer number, and it is going up.

## What is a wrench attack?

The name comes from an old joke about cryptography: there is no need to break the encryption if you can threaten the person who knows the password. It is the one attack the mathematics does nothing about. [A signature proves the key approved](/blog/how-transactions-are-signed/). It cannot show whether the hand was free.

## Why are wrench attacks increasing?

The report does not name a single cause, and nobody has a complete one. Two things are plainly true. Self-custody means there is no bank to call and no transfer to reverse — which is the point of it, and also what makes a person with a key worth coercing. And such people have become easier to find: [leaked customer lists](/blog/trezor-shipmonk-data-breach/) tie names and home addresses to wallet purchases, and a public chain lets anyone who learns one of your addresses see what it holds.

## What actually reduces the risk?

Mostly things that are not technology.

- **Do not be known for it.** Do not discuss holdings, post balances, or publicly use an address that is linked to your savings.
- **Separate what you carry from what you own.** The wallet on your phone should hold spending money. An attacker can only take what can be signed for on the spot.
- **Put distance into the larger amount.** A key kept somewhere you are not, or an arrangement that needs more than one device or person, cannot be produced under pressure in a living room.
- **Keep purchases quiet.** Delivery addresses and phone numbers given to crypto companies end up in databases.
- **If it happens, comply.** No balance is worth a life, and none of the measures above work as heroics.

Nura has no account and no server holding your phrase, so there is no sign-up that puts your name on a list of its users. It holds the key on the device in your hand — which makes it a wallet to carry, and a reason to think about how much you carry in it.
