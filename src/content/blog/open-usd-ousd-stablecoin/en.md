Open USD, ticker OUSD, went live on 30 September 2026. Its founding partners are Coinbase, Mastercard, Shopify, Stripe and Visa. It is issued by Bridge, the stablecoin company Stripe bought in 2024; its reserves are held at BlackRock, Lead Bank and BNY Mellon; and the partners have committed more than a billion dollars of initial liquidity, as The Paypers reported on 1 October. It runs on four networks: Ethereum, Base, Solana and Tempo.

That is a heavier list of names than most stablecoins launch with. It answers one question well and leaves two others exactly where they were.

## Who stands behind OUSD?

The question a stablecoin has to answer is who owes you the dollar. Here the answer is unusually legible: a named issuer, named custodians for the reserves, and minting and redemption at one to one. But read who that is offered to. Minting and redeeming run through Stripe, Visa's stablecoin platform, Mastercard-owned BVNK and Coinbase. A person holding OUSD in their own wallet holds [a claim on an issuer](/blog/stablecoins-explained/), reached through whoever will exchange it for them.

## Is OUSD safe to hold in a self-custodial wallet?

Holding it yourself changes who controls the key. It does not change what the token is. On Ethereum and Base, OUSD is an ERC-20: [a balance kept in a contract](/blog/erc20-tokens-explained/) that the issuer deployed and administers. Your key decides whether your balance moves. The contract's owner decides the rules the balance lives under.

## How do I avoid fake OUSD tokens?

Every launch with this much attention is followed by tokens borrowing the name. Anyone can deploy a contract called "Open USD", and the ticker is not new either — an older, unrelated token has used OUSD for years.

- Take the contract address from the issuer's own site, never from a search result, a reply, or a token that appeared in your wallet.
- Check the network. The same name on four chains means four different contracts.
- Treat an OUSD balance you did not expect as an advertisement, not as money.

Nura sends and receives ERC-20 tokens on the networks you switch between, so a token like this is one more contract address to it. It does not vouch for any token, and the recovery phrase that controls your balance stays encrypted on your device either way.

A long list of backers tells you who made the promise. It is still worth knowing that it is one.
