USDT is going back to Bitcoin. Utexo, an infrastructure company backed by Tether, says it will launch the stablecoin on the Bitcoin network in October 2026 using the RGB protocol, under a commercial licence to issue it with Tether's trademark, as Cryptonomist reported on 2 October. Tether's chief executive greeted the news with three words: "It's coming home." USDT first appeared on Bitcoin in 2014, through the Omni protocol, before most of its activity moved to Tron and Ethereum.

For anyone holding USDT the practical consequence is small and easy to get wrong: there is one more network where a token with that name lives.

## Is USDT on Bitcoin the same as USDT on Ethereum?

It is the same promise from the same issuer, recorded in a different place. On Ethereum, USDT is an ERC-20 — [a row in a contract](/blog/erc20-tokens-explained/). On Bitcoin under RGB, the asset is tracked by the people who hold it and anchored to Bitcoin transactions. The two do not see each other. A balance on one is not a balance on the other.

## What happens if I send USDT to the wrong network?

The address formats differ enough that many mistakes fail before they are sent. The expensive ones happen at the edges: withdrawing from an exchange and choosing the wrong network from a list, or giving a sender an address from a wallet that does not support the network they use. Moving value from one network to another is never a transfer. It is [a bridge or an exchange](/blog/bridging-between-chains/), with the trust that involves.

## What to check before you send or receive

- Ask which network, not just which token. "USDT" is no longer a complete answer.
- Confirm your wallet supports that network before you share an address.
- Send a small amount first whenever the route is new.
- Remember the token has an issuer. Utexo says it will blacklist the specific outputs tied to sanctioned or illicit activity rather than freeze whole addresses — a different mechanism, and [still somebody's promise](/blog/stablecoins-explained/).

Nura is an Ethereum wallet. It sends and receives native coins and ERC-20 tokens on the networks you switch between, so the USDT it shows you is the ERC-20 kind. USDT issued on Bitcoin through RGB is a different asset on a different network, and a Nura address is not somewhere to receive it.

The name on the token is the last thing to check, not the first.
